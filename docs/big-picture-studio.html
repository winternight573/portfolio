Big Picture Studio

A cooperative puzzle-narrative experience spread across four mobile screens, where players physically rearrange devices to uncover a shared world.

Role Game Designer, Technical Producer
Team CMU ETC Big Picture Studio
Tools Unity, Mobile Development, Figma, Playtesting
Links Site · Demo

01 Overview

Big Picture Studio explored a unique interaction space: a game played across four mobile phones that could be physically rearranged throughout play.

Unlike traditional mobile games, players were asked to think beyond the boundaries of a single screen. Phones could connect, separate, rotate, and move, transforming physical relationships between devices into puzzle mechanics and narrative moments.

As a game designer and technical producer, I focused on understanding how players learned this unfamiliar interaction language. Through onboarding design, puzzle structure exploration, playtesting, and experience flow analysis, I helped investigate how a completely new interaction space could become understandable, intuitive, and emotionally engaging.

02 Designing for an Unfamiliar Medium

The central design challenge was balancing familiarity and novelty.

Players already have deeply ingrained expectations about how phones work. At the same time, our experience asked them to treat four phones as a single shared system. Much of our design work centered on understanding which expectations to preserve and which expectations to challenge.

03 Building an Interaction Language

Before players could solve puzzles, they first needed to understand the rules of the medium itself.

I spent much of the semester helping break down interaction states and transitions into understandable components, identifying what players could currently do, what information they needed next, and how the experience should communicate new possibilities.

04 Designing Puzzle Progression

Because the interaction space itself was unfamiliar, puzzle progression needed to carefully manage complexity.

I explored lock-and-key relationships between mechanics, narrative beats, and player understanding to identify structures that could scale while remaining understandable.

05 Learning Through Playtesting

Playtesting became our primary design tool.

Since there were few existing references for four-device interaction design, many questions could only be answered by observing real players and studying where expectations aligned — or conflicted — with our intended experience.

Observation Design Response
Players understood screen movement before understanding screen connection. Adjusted onboarding sequence to isolate concepts.
Players often overlooked certain transitions. Added stronger visual communication between devices.

06 Producing Within Ambiguity

Big Picture Studio was a highly autonomous pitch team composed of designers, artists, programmers, and storytellers with strong creative ownership.

Unlike many projects where responsibilities are clearly separated, ideas frequently evolved through collective iteration and discussion.

Working within this environment challenged many of my assumptions about production and taught me valuable lessons about collaboration, communication, and adapting design processes to fit the needs of a team.

07 Communicating the Project

I maintained the project website as the primary public-facing record of the team's work. Weekly updates documented our evolving design process and helped communicate the interaction space to audiences who could not directly experience the game.

08 Final Experience

Through months of experimentation, iteration, and collaboration, the project culminated in a complete cooperative puzzle-narrative experience that invited players to see familiar devices in entirely new ways.

Reflection

Big Picture Studio taught me that designing a new interaction space is as much about understanding player expectations as it is about inventing new mechanics. The project also challenged me to reconsider how design ownership works within highly autonomous teams. While my contributions often focused on frameworks, playtesting, and experience structure rather than final content creation, I came away with a much deeper appreciation for how shared design conversations shape successful experiences.
